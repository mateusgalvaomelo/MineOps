# Operational mine map and directional coverage

The map has an original, fully local mine scene: open-pit benches, synthetic terrain contours, processing buildings, maintenance/staging facilities, reservoir and haul/access roads. These vector geometries are generated independently; they are not satellite imagery or a surveyed real mine.

Each LTE site's three cell azimuths produce rounded directional petals. The serving footprint stored in PostGIS is the valid union of the three lobes, joined at the antenna. This same footprint is used by expansion coverage assessment. Migration `004` updates existing circular footprints without resetting incidents or inventory. New sites use directional footprints immediately.

Each sector has three nested illustrative bands: strong (340m, nominal −80dBm), moderate (620m, −95dBm), edge (900m, −110dBm). Bearings are north-clockwise and positions convert nominal meters to local WGS84 degrees. Values are geometry/display assumptions, not measured signal or a terrain-aware RF prediction. Offline footprints show red and do not imply service; degraded footprints show amber. Selecting a site highlights its sectors. Sector buttons filter to a specific azimuth, while "Selected coverage only" isolates the complete selected footprint.

Site markers use antenna glyphs, status colors, identifiers and keyboard-accessible buttons. Focus centers and tilts the view; expand opens the map within the viewport; navigation, metric scale and a live WGS84 cursor coordinate readout support spatial inspection. The map shares inventory/alarms/devices with the Impact Analysis panel and adoption workflow.

Gold dashed outlines mark four illustrative excavation zones referenced to the visible benches and pits in public World Imagery. These are approximate visual annotations, not surveyed property or legal mining boundaries. The 24 fictional towers are positioned over exposed excavation areas rather than the earlier forest-side rectangle. Migration `006` updates areas, sites, current device positions, routes, coverage and request coordinates while preserving entity IDs and operational transactions. Historic telemetry remains a synthetic demonstration, not a record of that real mine's infrastructure.

**Map options** is closed by default. Open it to select the base, turn layers on/off, enable legends, or choose **No background** / **Hide all layers**. Close it with X or Escape. Coverage and devices are initially hidden to keep the aerial view readable; selecting an antenna sector turns its coverage on. **Hide map** closes the whole map while keeping site details/Impact Analysis, and **Show operational map** restores it. Background selection is remembered in the browser.

**Fit all towers** frames the complete operation and current sites, returns to a flat north-up camera and reserves room for map controls. A resize observer updates the map when its container changes between desktop, tablet and expanded view. Marker DOM nodes are reused across telemetry updates, avoiding flicker and lost keyboard focus. Explicit focus/zoom remains available; it intentionally shows a local view until Fit all towers is used.

**Signal probe** enables point inspection: click the mine map or an antenna to query the backend's coverage geometry. It reports the best available nominal RSRP band, serving candidate, sector/azimuth and number of overlapping active footprints. Offline site footprints are excluded from active service. This is explicitly a geometric simulation estimate; it does not measure actual signal. The probe remains linked to live query invalidation after incident changes.

An optional **OpenStreetMap · online** background supplies real street geography beneath the fictional mine overlay. It is enabled only by the user, fetched directly by the browser with normal caching, and displays visible OpenStreetMap attribution. The local mine background remains available without external tiles. No prefetch, offline tile downloads or tile scraping is implemented. Usage follows the [OSMF tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/) and [OpenStreetMap attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright). The online background does not identify the fictional assets as a real mine.

The new MineOps mark is an original vector haul truck, reused in the sidebar and favicon. It introduces mining identity without adding bitmap assets or external fonts.

**Satellite · Esri · online** loads World Imagery directly from the public Esri tile service, with visible attribution to Esri, Vantor, Earthstar Geographics and the GIS User Community. This option requires internet and retains the [upstream World Imagery terms](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=10df2279f9684e4a9f6a7f08febac2a9). No imagery is bundled, prefetched or exported as offline tiles. Satellite/street modes hide generated local landforms and buildings so they do not obscure the actual background, while the fictional boundary, LTE sites, devices and coverage remain visible. The imagery is real geography; the mine and LTE assets are fictional overlays.
